# Howl design direction

**Chosen source:** Round 02, version B — “Bigger Beat,” refined in [the linked Figma frame](https://www.figma.com/design/uBs510XetOVzImw8Va7nob/Howl-explorations?node-id=9-2&t=Nhx9RNLW0wGMAwkh-4). The frame visibly presents **B / Open Mic**. This direction carries forward Round 01’s Open Mic foundation and Round 02B’s more prominent, readable post text; the refinement shown in Figma is the final visual reference.

## The feeling

Loud and fun at a glance, welcoming and calm once you start reading. Howl should feel like an open campus conversation: the brand has energy, but student posts—not interface decoration—get the attention. Keep the feed airy, clear, and easy to scan on a phone.

## Visual language

- **Color:** Deep slate `#17202B` frames a clean white `#FFFFFF` reading surface on wider screens. Use Howl violet `#C014FF` for the wordmark, active state, and a few clear actions. Set primary copy in near-black `#20232A`; use muted gray `#686570` for metadata and pale gray `#E2E4E8` for dividers. Soft, varied avatar tints add small touches of color.
- **Type:** Use Georgia (with a serif fallback) for the expressive brand and feed headline. Use Arial/Helvetica (or a sans-serif fallback) for post copy, labels, and controls. Keep post text at the selected B scale—19px with 1.5 line-height—so longer campus stories remain comfortable to read.
- **Shapes and layout:** Favor a simple white feed column, subtle 8px desktop corners, thin separators, and generous space around posts. Use small circular avatars and a quiet outlined heart. On phones, let the feed use the available width and keep the compact navigation within easy reach.

## Do

1. Let student stories lead; keep branding bold but brief.
2. Preserve readable post size, line spacing, and clear author/meta hierarchy.
3. Use violet selectively to identify Howl and show the current action or section.
4. Separate posts with whitespace and light rules rather than heavy containers.
5. Keep touch controls comfortable and the feed layout clear on small screens.

## Don’t

1. Don’t shrink post copy to fit more items on screen.
2. Don’t turn every label, icon, or control violet.
3. Don’t add heavy shadows, thick borders, or stacked decorative cards.
4. Don’t let secondary metadata compete with the post itself.
5. Don’t make the mobile feed feel cramped or bury its navigation.
